🔁 The Question

Take something, carry it through a long loop of changes, and bring it back to where it started. Is it "the same thing"? Distinction Holonomy takes that old philosophical chestnut and gives it a mathematical shape borrowed from gauge theory: transport a thing around a closed loop of transformations and compare what comes back to what went in. The mismatch — if there is one — is the holonomy.

🧵 Why This Beats "Just Compare Snapshots"

Comparing a before-picture and an after-picture throws away the path. Two objects can look identical at both ends and still have gone through completely different repairs, detours, or near-misses in between — and some of that history keeps mattering even after the surface looks settled again. Holonomy keeps the path in the picture on purpose.

🩹 Repair, Not Restoration

One of the punchlines: a gauge-invariant notion of "repair" doesn't mean "put it back exactly as it was." It means finding a loop-consistent way to keep going that doesn't quietly smuggle in a contradiction later. Something can be fully, honestly repaired and still not be a byte-for-byte restoration — and that's not a failure of the repair, that's just what continuation actually looks like.
