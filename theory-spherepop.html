🎮 Four Buttons That Run Everything

Spherepop is a tiny operator calculus built from exactly four moves. The claim is that a surprising amount of "what happens next" — in computer systems, in physics, in reasoning — reduces to some combination of these:

POP
introduce something new
REFUSE
rule an option out
BIND
couple things together
COLLAPSE
commit to one outcome

🖥️ Why an Operating System?

Take those four primitives seriously and you can sketch an entire OS on top of them — one where the fundamental record isn't files and processes but an append-only log of events. Nothing is silently overwritten; history is retained, and "deleting" something just means appending a note that it's no longer reachable. Everything else — forking a process, mounting a filesystem, scheduling a task — turns out to be a named recipe built out of Pop/Refuse/Bind/Collapse rather than a fifth fundamental move.

🧩 The Fun Part

Proving that claim means going through ordinary OS features one at a time — renaming a file, mounting a drive, waiting on a child process — and either successfully rebuilding them from the four primitives, or discovering exactly where the four primitives run out of room. Both outcomes are useful. The second one tells you something real about the limits of the claim instead of quietly hiding it.
