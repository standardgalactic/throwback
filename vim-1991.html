VI (1976) → VIM (1991): A REAL, TINY EMULATOR

vi was written by Bill Joy in 1976 for BSD Unix, for a world of slow serial terminals where every keystroke sent over the wire was expensive — hence a language of single-letter commands and no menus. Vim ("Vi IMproved") followed in 1991 and is still, thirty-plus years later, what half the internet's servers get edited with over SSH. The best way to learn it isn't to read about it — it's to type into it. Below is a small but real modal editor, implemented in JavaScript, with six guided lessons.

Lesson

NORMAL

Click the black terminal above, then type. It captures your keyboard the way a real terminal window would — click elsewhere (or press Esc then Tab) to escape it.
